What the work clarified

Decision support, not false certainty.

The package is useful because it preserves multiple paths while isolating the assumptions that still need real-world confirmation.

01

Multiple exit paths remain visible

The package keeps the major possibilities understandable rather than prematurely locking the property into one story: sell, separate value, retain a buildable portion, or prepare a future development path.

02

Boundary and access questions are isolated

The work identified the narrow frontage/boundary condition near the garage and the importance of resolving it before treating the eastern side as fully independent development ground.

03

Density and ADU concepts remain feasibility tools

Potential duplex/ADU ideas can guide questions and valuations, but they are not being presented as permits, survey conclusions, or guaranteed entitlements.

The next value-producing steps

Move from model to verified facts.

These are the next conversations and professional checks that can materially change the property decision.

1 · Neighbor
Resolve frontage intent. Confirm whether the neighboring owner is willing to cooperate on the small frontage/boundary adjustment before paying for an elaborate plan built on an assumption.
2 · Survey
Turn geometry into recordable facts. Survey the relevant boundary and access condition so later title, City, and development conversations share the same base information.
3 · Title
Review the actual recorded instruments. Public summaries are not enough for the unresolved ownership/boundary lane.
4 · City
Confirm the planning path. Use the concept package to ask focused questions about lot treatment, density, access, parking, ADU constraints, and boundary adjustment.
5 · Lender
Protect the financial exit. Confirm how any split, sale, cash-back structure, or retained parcel interacts with the existing loan.
6 · Utilities
Price hidden infrastructure. Determine service availability and separation costs before assigning development value to the vacant side.

What not to do yet

Preserve optionality.

The current work is strong enough to guide the next steps, but not to substitute for the authorities that own the final facts.

A

Do not price the vacant side from zoning alone.

Buildable value depends on access, utilities, survey, title, setbacks, City interpretation, and financing—not just theoretical density.

B

Do not record a boundary idea before the whole path is checked.

A small geometry change can create larger effects for title, setbacks, lender collateral, or future development.

C

Do not let the website become the decision maker.

The digital package organizes evidence and conversations. Legal, survey, lending, and City authorities remain outside the software.

Recommended next move

Spend the next dollars on facts that change the decision.

Neighbor/frontage intent, survey, title, City direction, lender constraints, and utilities now have more decision value than another round of concept polish. Once those facts are in hand, the sale-versus-retain-versus-develop options can be compared on firmer ground.
